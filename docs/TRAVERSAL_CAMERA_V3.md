# Predictive traversal camera V3

The shared `TraversalCameraRig` predicts the next reachable lower support from the live player body and authored collision surfaces. It uses current gravity, horizontal speeds, dash state, moving-surface phase, wind, and trap timing. Normal traversal remains a camera no-op. Active framing solves the follow offset against the player body center and existing deadzone, then applies a relative, uniform zoom; the HUD camera is unchanged.

Grounded predictions begin within `clamp(abs(commanded speed) * 1.1 seconds, 160, 320)` world units of a support edge. The 1.1-second horizon gives camera easing and native follow smoothing time to settle. Framing uses a 20-logical-pixel interior margin inside the accepted vertical band 120–370. At each candidate zoom (1.0, .85, .80), the solver now checks vertical feasibility and the *prospectively projected* horizontal touchdown width and associated hazard visibility using that ratio's wider world view and unchanged horizontal center. It reports invisible if no ratio fits.

Jail, Outside, and Crimson use live chapter surfaces and trap providers. Castle uses actual collision pieces. Furnace uses its entry, step, and exit bodies, moving lift/ferry phase, conveyor drift, active heat tells, and basin hazards. Pit risk uses the kill line rather than an entire floor-to-bed column. The Furnace route tests independently verify all 79 authored jump edges at 30, 60, and 120 Hz, including safe launch windows; the current whole-route camera audit checks all 15 authored lower step pairs whose next step is at least 48 units lower, and requires each forecast to land on that immediate next real surface. The new room 9 step 0→1 test checks its long crossing against the unchanged horizontal follow projection at walk and sprint speed across 30/60/120 Hz, classifying it as normal-camera visible before takeoff.

The generated geometry audit for Outside and Crimson evaluates every adjacent authored step pair whose next step is more than 48 units lower, using actual source and first-intercept surface IDs, current spike placement, quality-room pit kill lines, and the camera projection. It fails on unclassified `none`, `danger`, or non-landing outcomes instead of silently skipping them. Jail has a separate 18-link route audit, explicit neutral step handling, pit geometry, and a spike-risk case. These deterministic audits cover authored route geometry and first interceptions; they do not constitute a live simulation of every gate puzzle state, shutter/crumble timer phase, moving-surface phase, enemy collision, or full player input sequence. Moving traps, heat cycles, and Furnace route windows have separate tests. No claim of complete all-state traversal proof is made.

Root-run browser evidence reported on 2026-10-12: Outside room 16 step 3 produced a 633 ms sprint lead; Castle support 19 produced a 575 ms first-drop lead; Furnace room 8 consecutive links produced 750/433 ms, and room 9 consecutive links 683/508 ms. Root also checked Regent camera ownership and Jail at 390×844 and 844×390. The Furnace room 9 step 0→1 screenshot was captured after takeoff, so it is not counted as live prelaunch evidence; the new automated projection test is geometry evidence only. The temporary fixture is archived outside the production public directory at `outputs/predictive-camera/fixture/parkour-camera-qa.html` in the parent workspace. It can be restored locally for further review.

The fixture is local-development-only, starts with damage ignored, latches readiness after the selected real support contact and camera setup, and records full post-render traces with visible landing IDs, world view, actor screen bounds, and risk bounds. QA runs use `devPreview` and do not persist campaign progress. Tests and builds are not a full user playthrough.

Final local verification: 522 tests across 73 files and the TypeScript/Vite production build passed. Root additionally observed Furnace room 9 step 0→1 becoming visible during the real sprint approach, before leaving the source collision body, and checked pause/resume continuity. This short-runway browser check does not establish a 300 ms launch lead for that crossing. No publication has been performed. Human campaign playtesting remains necessary.
