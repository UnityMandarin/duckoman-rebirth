# Replay and discovery design

This phase adds a persistent field journal, boss practice trials, and a compact record of trial results. The journal keeps undiscovered entries limited to a neutral “Undiscovered” label so exploration reveals the clue and its story in play. Practice trials give players a repeatable way to learn boss patterns without changing campaign state; time, accepted damage events, and a hitless mark make improvement visible.

The design is informed by three references, without treating them as proof that any feature will produce a particular outcome:

- [Ryan, Rigby, and Przybylski, “The Motivational Pull of Video Games”](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8) connects autonomy and competence with enjoyment and future play. Optional trials support player choice; records provide clear evidence of improvement.
- [Sweetser and Wyeth, “GameFlow”](https://doi.org/10.1145/1077246.1077253) describes challenge, player skills, clear goals, and feedback among its elements of enjoyable play. A repeatable boss encounter and concise result screen apply those ideas to this campaign.
- [Hollow Knight: Silksong](https://hollowknightsilksong.com/) is a craft benchmark for a hand-made 2D world built around movement mastery, enemies, and discoveries. It is a reference for the intended exploration mood, not a claim that this work matches its quality.
